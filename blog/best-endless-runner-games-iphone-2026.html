The Best Endless Runner Games on iPhone and iPad in 2026 (Including Ones Without Ads)

Published 26 October 2026 · 8 min read · Games

The endless runner is one of the oldest ideas in mobile gaming, and it still works. A character runs forward on their own, you tap or swipe to dodge, and the only question is how far you can get before something catches you. There is no tutorial to sit through and no story you have to follow. You can play for ninety seconds in a queue and feel like you did something.

The trouble is what often surrounds that simple loop. Most of the best-known runners on iPhone and iPad are free to download and paid for with advertising and in-app purchases, and because every run ends quickly, the interruptions come quickly too. This guide looks at the genre fairly: what the big names do well, what you pay for them one way or another, and how to play a runner without ads if that matters to you.

What Makes a Good Endless Runner?

On paper every runner is the same game. In practice the good ones get a handful of things right.

  • Controls that disappear. The best runners work with one thumb, on a phone held in one hand. If you are thinking about the buttons, the game has already lost you.
  • Short, fair runs. A run should end because you made a mistake, not because the game threw something unavoidable at you. When you fail, you should know why, and want to go again straight away.
  • Risk against reward. Coins and bonuses sit just outside the safe line. Greed is the genre’s main source of tension, and good level patterns let you choose how greedy to be.
  • Progress between runs. Upgrades, characters, missions or new places to run through give a bad run some value. Without them, a failed attempt is just a failed attempt.
  • A reason to come back tomorrow. Daily goals, a world that changes, bosses or unlockables keep the loop from going stale after a week.

Most of the disagreement between players is about that last pair. Some people want a deep upgrade tree and a constant stream of new things. Others want to run, die, and run again without being asked for anything.

The Big Names, Fairly

We checked the App Store listings and Apple’s own pages for the runners most people mention first (checked October 2026, US store). All of them are well made, and the longest-running ones have years of polish behind them.

Subway Surfers

The best-known runner on the App Store. You slide between train lines, dodge barriers and collect coins as a graffiti kid being chased by an inspector. It is free with in-app purchases, and the listing says it contains advertising. Coin and key packs on the page run from $0.99 to $4.99. It works on iPhone and iPad and the download is about 411 MB. Its strengths are bright, readable visuals and enormous popularity, which means plenty of other players to compare scores with. There is also a separate Subway Surfers+ on Apple Arcade, which Apple describes as having no ads and no in-app purchases.

Temple Run 2

The game that made the genre mainstream. You swipe to turn, jump and slide while fleeing through ruins, and the pace and the fear of the next corner still hold up. It is free with in-app purchases from $0.99 up to $19.99, and the listing says it contains advertising. It runs on iPhone and iPad and is about 263 MB, which makes it one of the smaller downloads here. Its strength is simplicity: it is as easy to learn today as it was on day one.

Jetpack Joyride

The long-running classic of the jetpack runner. You hold to fly, let go to fall and weave through lasers and missiles while collecting coins and gadgets. It is free with in-app purchases from $0.99 to $29.99, and the listing says it contains advertising. It runs on iPhone, iPad and Apple TV and is about 418 MB. Its great strength is personality: vehicles, costumes and a constant stream of silly ideas. An ad-free Jetpack Joyride+ has been part of Apple Arcade since 2021.

Alto’s Odyssey

The calm one. You snowboard across desert dunes at dusk, and the point is flow rather than panic. The version on the App Store today is Alto’s Odyssey — Remastered, which is an Apple Arcade game: the page says no ads and no in-app purchases, and it plays on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Vision and Apple TV. The download is about 444 MB and the age rating is 4+. If your idea of a runner is closer to a screensaver you can play, this is the reference point.

Sonic Dash+

A fast, bright runner with a famous character, and the Apple Arcade version has been described since its April 2022 launch as having no ads and no in-app purchases. We could not confirm the current state of the free version, so we have left it out of the table below.

Why Ads Hurt Runners More Than Most Genres

An endless runner is built around failure. You will lose, usually within a couple of minutes, and then you want to start again. That moment is exactly where an advertisement fits most easily, which is why the free versions tend to put a screen, a video or an offer to continue right between one run and the next.

None of this is a scandal. Free games have to be paid for somehow, and a full-screen ad is a fair price for some people. But it does change how the game feels. A runner is at its best when the gap between failing and trying again is about one second, and every extra screen in that gap makes the loop slightly less addictive and slightly more of a chore.

There is also the question of what the game is built to sell. If the progression is designed around coins that run out, the design pushes you toward the shop. That is worth watching for when you compare listings.

How to Play an Endless Runner Without Ads

You have more options than you might think.

1. Use the Apple Arcade version

If you already subscribe, several of the best-known runners have an ad-free edition, such as Subway Surfers+, Jetpack Joyride+, Alto’s Odyssey — Remastered and Sonic Dash+. They are the same kind of game with the ads and shop removed. The cost is the subscription itself, and the games stay available only while you subscribe.

2. Read what “remove ads” actually removes

Some free games sell an ad-free option. Before paying, read the purchase description, and check whether optional “watch a video to continue” offers go away as well. Know what you are buying.

3. Look for the label on the store page

The App Store page lists “Contains Advertising” among its age-rating details for games that show ads, and the App Privacy section lists data used to track you. Both are quick to read. If the page says nothing about advertising and lists no tracking, the game is a much safer bet if you want to be left alone.

4. Try offline play

Some ad-supported games show fewer ads when there is no connection. Others refuse to start or hold back rewards. Airplane Mode costs nothing to test, though a game that only works well offline is hardly an ad-free game.

5. Choose a game that is free and does not run ads

A smaller group of games is free to try, has no advertising at all, and makes its money from optional purchases that do not change how hard the game is. They are rarer, and usually from smaller studios, but they exist.

It is free to play, with no ads, no energy timers, no tracking and no account, and your progress stays on your device. Tips to Run Farther in Any Runner

These apply to almost every game in this guide, whatever its theme.

Look ahead, not at your character

Your eyes should sit well ahead of your character, on what is about to arrive. By the time something reaches your character it is too late to react, so reading the next obstacle is most of the skill.

Treat coins as optional

The most common way to end a good run is a coin placed just outside the safe line. A coin you leave behind costs you nothing, and a run that ends early costs you every coin after it. Take the risky ones only when the way ahead is quiet.

Learn the patterns

Runners build their levels from a limited set of obstacle layouts. After a few hours you will recognise most of them, and your hands will respond before you consciously decide. That is why beginners die in seconds and regulars last minutes.

Spend upgrades early on what keeps you alive

If a game lets you earn upgrades, put the first ones into anything that gives you a second chance or a longer window to react, rather than into a bigger coin multiplier. A longer run earns more coins than a better multiplier on a short one.

Keep sessions short

Runners reward alertness. If you notice you are dying to things you would have dodged an hour ago, stop for a while. A rested player beats a tired one with a better phone.

Use missions and goals as direction

A mission such as “collect a set number of coins in one run” gives a bad attempt a purpose and pushes you to try styles you would otherwise skip. They are a quiet way to improve.

Which Runner Is Right for You?

  • You want the biggest community and the most content: Subway Surfers and Temple Run 2 have years of updates behind them. If you can live with ads, they remain excellent, and you can pay to reduce the interruptions.
  • You want personality and gadgets: Jetpack Joyride is hard to beat for silliness and variety, in either its free or Apple Arcade form.
  • You want to relax: Alto’s Odyssey is the calm end of the genre and has no ads, if you are happy with an Apple Arcade subscription.
  • You want a free runner without ads or a subscription: look for a game with no advertising listed on its page, optional purchases that do not alter difficulty, and no account. Read the newest reviews before committing.

An endless runner is at its best when you can pick it up for a minute and put it down again with no cost and no interruption. Whichever one you choose, pick a game that lets you stay in that rhythm instead of breaking it every couple of runs.
