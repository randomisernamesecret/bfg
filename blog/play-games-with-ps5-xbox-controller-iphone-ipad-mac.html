How to Play iPhone, iPad and Mac Games With a PS5 or Xbox Controller

October 2026 · 8 min read · Games

Touch controls have come a long way, but there is a reason so many people keep a controller in the drawer next to the television. A physical stick and real buttons give you precision, comfort and a clear view of the screen. The good news is that the same PlayStation or Xbox controller you already own works with an iPhone, an iPad and a Mac, and setting it up is simple.

This guide covers which controllers work, how to pair them step by step, how to switch between touch and controller without fuss, and the small habits that make longer sessions more comfortable. Along the way we will show what a controller changes in three of our own games.

Why a Controller Helps in Action and Racing Games

Not every game benefits from a controller. A puzzle game or a card game is usually better with your finger on the screen. Fast action and racing games are different, for three practical reasons.

Precise movement

A virtual joystick has no edges. Your thumb drifts a little off centre, the character drifts with it, and you only notice when something hits you. A physical stick springs back to the middle every time you let go, and you can feel exactly how far you have pushed it. In a horde game, where survival depends on threading a gap between enemies, or in a racer, where a corner rewards a smooth line, that tactile feedback is worth a great deal.

Your thumbs are off the action

On a phone, your thumbs sit on top of the very part of the screen you need to watch. Enemies appear under your knuckles, and the edge of a track hides behind your hand. With a controller the whole screen is visible all the time. That matters most on an iPhone, where every centimetre of display counts.

Longer, more comfortable sessions

Holding a phone up with both hands while pressing on glass tires the wrists. A controller lets you sit back, rest your arms and put the screen at a comfortable distance on a stand or a table. Many players find they make fewer mistakes late in a run simply because they are not tense.

Which Controllers Work

Three families of controllers work well with iPhone, iPad and Mac:

  • PlayStation 5 DualSense controller. The standard DualSense and the DualSense Edge both pair over Bluetooth.
  • Xbox Wireless Controller. The current Xbox Series X|S controller and later Xbox One controllers with Bluetooth pair directly. Very early Xbox One controllers without Bluetooth do not.
  • MFi-certified controllers. Many third-party pads, including grip-style controllers that hold the phone between two handles, are certified for Apple devices. Look for “Made for iPhone” or “MFi” on the box.

The PlayStation 4 DualShock 4 also pairs with Apple devices, though it is now an older model. If you are buying a controller specifically for your iPhone or iPad, one of the options above is the safer choice.

How to Pair a PS5 DualSense Controller

  1. Open Bluetooth settings. On iPhone or iPad, go to Settings → Bluetooth and make sure Bluetooth is on. On a Mac, open System Settings → Bluetooth.
  2. Put the controller into pairing mode. With the controller switched off, press and hold the PS button and the Create button (the small button to the left of the touchpad) together until the light bar around the touchpad starts flashing rapidly.
  3. Select it. “DualSense Wireless Controller” appears in the list of other devices. Tap or click it.
  4. Confirm. The light bar turns solid and the player indicator lights up. You are connected.

From then on, pressing the PS button reconnects the controller to the last device it was paired with, as long as that device has Bluetooth turned on.

How to Pair an Xbox Wireless Controller

  1. Open Bluetooth settings on your iPhone, iPad or Mac, as above.
  2. Turn the controller on by pressing the Xbox button.
  3. Hold the pair button. This is the small button on the top edge of the controller, next to the charging port. Hold it for a few seconds until the Xbox button starts flashing quickly.
  4. Select it. “Xbox Wireless Controller” appears in the list. Tap or click it, and the Xbox button stays lit once the connection is made.
If the controller does not appear: switch it off, wait a moment and try pairing mode again. A slow flash usually means it is trying to find a device it already knows; a fast flash means it is ready to pair with something new.

Menus you can drive from the sofa

In all three games, every menu can be navigated with the controller. Supported controllers rumble on the big moments, so you feel a boss arriving or a heavy hit landing without taking your eyes off the action. And if the controller disconnects mid-run, because the battery runs out or someone walks off with it, the game pauses straight away rather than leaving your character standing still in the middle of a fight.

Switching Between Touch and Controller

You do not have to choose one input method for good. Touch controls keep working while a controller is connected, and touching the screen switches back to touch controls instantly. Put the controller down to answer a message, pick the phone up and carry on with your thumbs, then reach for the controller again when you sit back down. There is no setting to change and no menu to visit.

This is also handy when you share a device. One person can play on the train with touch, and another can play the same game at home with a controller, without anyone reconfiguring anything.

Practical Tips for Playing With a Controller

Keep it charged

Wireless controllers rarely warn you loudly before they run out. Charge yours after a long session, and keep a USB-C cable near where you play. On iPhone and iPad, the Batteries widget shows the charge level of a connected controller, which is a quick way to check before you start a long run.

Re-pair after using it with a console

A controller can only remember one active connection at a time. If you use your DualSense with a PS5, or your Xbox controller with an Xbox, it will usually reconnect to the console the next time you press its home button. When you want it back on your phone or tablet, put it into pairing mode again and select it in Bluetooth settings. When you return it to the console, connecting it with a cable is often the quickest way to switch it back.

Use a phone clip or a stand

On an iPhone, a phone clip that attaches to the top of the controller turns it into a compact handheld. Choose one that grips firmly and lets you tilt the phone so you are not looking down at a steep angle. If you would rather spare your hands the weight, a simple desk stand works just as well, especially for longer sessions.

Prop up the iPad

An iPad on a stand or a folding case at arm’s length is one of the most comfortable ways to play. The screen is big enough to read every detail of a crowded fight or a tight street circuit, and your hands rest in your lap. Landscape games such as Bellgrave Holdout are especially well suited to this setup.

On a Mac

Bellgrave also runs on the Mac, where a controller is often more comfortable than a keyboard for a game built around movement and dodging. Pair it in System Settings → Bluetooth exactly as you would on an iPhone.

Mind the battery on your device as well

Bluetooth uses very little power, but long gaming sessions still drain an iPhone. If you plan to play for a while, keep the phone on charge or start with a healthy battery.

Quick Troubleshooting

  • The controller connects but nothing happens in the game. Make sure it is not also connected to another nearby device, such as a console or a second iPad. Turn Bluetooth off on the other device, or forget the controller there.
  • Input feels delayed. Move closer to the device and remove anything solid between them. Lots of other wireless gadgets nearby can also cause interference.
  • The controller keeps disconnecting. Charge it fully, then in Bluetooth settings choose “Forget This Device” and pair it again from scratch.
  • The wrong button prompts appear. Disconnect any other controllers so only the one you are holding is paired.

A Better Way to Play, Without Extra Baggage

A controller is one of the simplest upgrades you can make to mobile gaming. If you already own a console, you probably have one in the house; pairing is simple, and it changes how fast action and racing games feel.
