PhotoFlight vs Polarsteps (2026): Live Travel Tracking or a Map From Your Photos?

September 2026 · 9 min read · Travel

Polarsteps and PhotoFlight both end with a map of where you have been, but they get there from opposite directions. Polarsteps follows you while you travel and turns each trip into a shareable journal. PhotoFlight looks backwards at the photos already in your library and rebuilds the trips you have taken over the years. PhotoFlight is our app, so we have tried to be fair: this comparison explains how each one works in 2026, what it costs, where your data lives, and which suits which kind of traveller.

How Polarsteps Works

Polarsteps is built around the trip you are about to take. You create an account, plan a trip, and while it is active the app uses your phone's location in the background to draw your route and drop "steps" where you stop. You add photos and notes to each step, and friends and family can follow along. When you get home you have a polished timeline with a route line on a map.

Two extras stand out. Polarsteps can turn a trip into a printed travel book, laid out automatically from your route, photos and notes, which makes a lovely keepsake or gift. And it has a large community: you can follow other travellers, share trips publicly or keep them private.

Planning, tracking and sharing trips are free. Printed books cost extra, and in July 2026 Polarsteps added Polarsteps Plus, an optional subscription with 3D maps, extra map styles, a mode that replays your journey, more detailed step statistics and a discount on books. Because trips live in your Polarsteps account, they sync between devices and can be shared, which also means your travel history is stored on the company's servers.

How PhotoFlight Works

PhotoFlight does not track anything while you travel. When you open it, it reads the dates and locations already stored in your photo library and works out where you have been: countries, cities, and multi-stop trips with dates and places. From that it builds a 3D globe, a world map, a travel passport with totals such as countries, continents, days away and distance, and albums for every trip, country and city.

It also estimates how you travelled between places. A flight is inferred only when your photos support it, for example when a photo was taken in the air, or when two photo locations are too far apart to have driven between them. PhotoFlight says plainly that routes and transport are estimates, not verified flight records, and you can correct dates, places and flights by hand. If you allow calendar access, flight bookings in your calendar can help confirm what it found.

A free Scan Report explains what the scan found and what is missing, such as photos without a location, and suggests matches where the evidence supports them. You can add your current and previous homes so everyday life does not count as travel, keep a bucket list of places you want to visit, and, with PhotoFlight Pro, turn a trip, country or city into a short portrait video with music.

PhotoFlight is free to download. The free version covers your recent history; PhotoFlight Pro is a one-time purchase that unlocks your whole photo history, the full trip timeline and albums, manual entries and corrections, share cards, videos, widgets and NFC souvenir links. There is no subscription and no account.

Prospective vs Retrospective

This is the real difference between the two, and it shapes everything else.

Polarsteps is prospective. It records forward from the moment you start a trip. The trip to Japan you took before installing it does not exist there unless you add it by hand from memory, without the automatic route that makes Polarsteps special.

PhotoFlight is retrospective. On first launch it can see every geotagged photo in your library, which for many people means ten years or more of travel. The Portugal road trip, the weekend in Edinburgh, the conference in Chicago: if you took photos, they appear on your map after the first scan.

The flip side is coverage. PhotoFlight only knows about places where you took a photo with location data. Polarsteps records your route whether or not you take pictures, and draws the road between stops rather than inferring it.

Where Polarsteps Wins

If you want a detailed record of one trip as it happens, Polarsteps is purpose-built for it. The recorded route shows the coastal road you drove and the ferry you took, not just the towns at either end. The step-by-step journal turns a map into a story: what you ate, who you met, what the weather did. Years later, those notes are often more evocative than the photos.

The printed books are genuinely hard to beat as a gift after a big trip, and the social side is strong. Family can follow a long journey in near real time, which many travellers value for peace of mind as much as for fun. And the core app costs nothing, so trying it is easy.

The battery question: Any app that records your route in the background uses some battery while you travel, just when you rely on your phone for maps, tickets and photos. Polarsteps works to keep that low, but an app that only reads photos you have already taken uses none while you are on the road.

Where PhotoFlight Wins

PhotoFlight's biggest advantage is time: not the time it takes, but the time it can see. If you have carried a smartphone for a decade, you get a decade of travel on your map without typing a thing. No "I wish I had started tracking earlier."

It also asks for very little. There is no trip to start, no account to create and nothing running in the background. Your travel history is built and stored on your device; Apple's services look up place names and draw the maps, and your photo library is never uploaded to us. That suits people who like the idea of a travel map but not of a company holding a record of their movements.

And it turns an overflowing camera roll into something you can browse. Albums for every trip, country and city, a passport of totals and milestones, and even small places counted properly (Monaco and Vatican City are not quietly folded into France and Italy) make it satisfying to scroll back through years of travel. If you prefer to pay once, Pro is a single purchase rather than a subscription.

Which Should You Choose?

Choose Polarsteps if:

  • You want a detailed, real-time journal of an upcoming trip, with notes at every stop
  • You want family and friends to follow along as you travel
  • You would love a printed book of the trip afterwards
  • You enjoy documenting each day as part of the experience
  • You are comfortable with an account and your trips stored online

Choose PhotoFlight if:

  • You want years of past trips on a map without entering anything
  • You would rather not run location tracking in the background while you travel
  • You want your travel history kept on your device, with no account
  • You like browsing albums, stats and short videos of trips you have already taken
  • You prefer a one-time purchase to a subscription for the extras

The two also work well together. Many travellers use PhotoFlight as the big-picture record of everywhere they have been, and switch on Polarsteps for the occasional big trip they want to journal in detail. The retrospective map and the live journal answer different questions.

Be honest about how you travel. If you know you will not write notes or remember to start a trip, Polarsteps' best features will go unused.
