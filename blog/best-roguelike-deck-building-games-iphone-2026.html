The Best Roguelike Deck-Building Card Games on iPhone and iPad in 2026

Published 26 October 2026 · 8 min read · Games

Deck-building roguelikes have quietly become one of the best things you can play on a phone. The idea is simple: you start with a small, weak set of cards, pick up better ones as a run goes on, and try to climb as far as you can before the run ends. Then you start again, a little wiser. Each run is short, no two are alike, and the pleasure comes from the deck you built rather than from fast thumbs.

If you are searching for one to play on iPhone or iPad, you will probably also care how it charges. Plenty of free card games wrap their cards in ads, waiting timers or a subscription. This guide looks at five well-known deck-builders and one smaller creature-collecting game, says what each does well, states how each is sold (checked October 2026), and finishes with practical advice on building a deck that actually wins.

What Is a Roguelike Deck-Builder?

The label covers a lot of different games, but most share the same three ingredients.

  • A deck that grows during the run. You begin with a handful of basic cards. After fights you are offered new cards, upgrades or relics, and every choice shapes what you can do later.
  • Turn-based fights with limited resources. You can only play so many cards per turn, so each turn is a small puzzle about what to play, what to hold back and what to ignore.
  • A run that can end. When you lose, you start over. What carries across is mostly what you have learned, plus the odd unlock.

That last point is what separates the genre from collectible card games, where your collection grows steadily and the deck is built once, between matches. In a roguelike you build while you play, so the skill is in making good choices under pressure and knowing when to say no to a tempting card.

Why Business Model Matters in a Card Game

Card games are a natural fit for mobile monetisation. Cards can be sold in packs, battles can be gated behind an energy bar, and a rewarded video can sit between every two fights. None of that is wrong in itself, and some players enjoy the collecting. But it changes how a game feels. A deck-builder you pay for once asks nothing of you after the purchase. A free one may be perfectly generous, or it may spend its time nudging you towards the next pack.

So before you download, it is worth asking three questions. Do you pay once, pay nothing up front, or subscribe? Does the game run on timers or energy? And are cards, or the power to win, something you can buy? The games below answer those questions differently, and none is the right answer for everyone.

The Well-Known Names, Fairly

Slay the Spire

The game that defined the genre. You climb a tower, choose a path between fights, shops and events, and assemble a deck with a distinct playstyle for each character. It is a universal app for iPhone and iPad, priced at $9.99 on the US App Store with no in-app purchases listed. It rewards patience and planning more than any other game here, and it is still the benchmark for how a run should feel. It is also the most demanding, so expect to lose a lot while you learn.

Balatro

Balatro takes poker hands and turns them into a deck-builder: you beat escalating Boss Blinds by scoring with strong hands, helped by more than 150 Jokers that bend the rules. It is $9.99 on the App Store for iPhone and iPad, and a separate Balatro+ version is included in Apple Arcade for subscribers. It is the most accessible game on this list if you already know poker, and probably the hardest to put down. Its pixel-art look and its sense of ever-escalating scores are a big part of the charm.

Monster Train

Monster Train asks you to defend a train across several floors, mixing two clans of creatures and spells, and it is built on tactical positioning as much as card choice. It launched on iOS in October 2022 as a premium game. On the US App Store we saw $7.99 with an optional add-on, though regional prices differ. It suits players who want depth and synergy and don’t mind a busier screen, and some reviewers note that its controls are happier on iPad than on a small phone.

Dicey Dungeons

Here the cards are replaced by dice, which you roll and slot into equipment to attack, defend and trigger effects. It is an approachable, colourful, quick game, listed at $4.99 on the US App Store for iPhone and iPad with no in-app purchases listed. If cards feel heavy and you want something lighter with a streak of mischief, this is the one to try.

Wildfrost

Wildfrost uses a different battle layout: your companions stand in a line, a countdown on each card decides when it acts, and a snowy world full of oddball characters surrounds it. It is free to download and you can play until you beat the first boss, after which the full game is a single $6.99 purchase on the US App Store. The free start makes it the easiest of the premium-style games to try before you commit.

How to Build a Deck That Wins

These ideas apply to almost every deck-builder, whichever you choose.

Take fewer cards than you think you should

New players grab every attractive card they are offered. A bloated deck draws its best cards less often. Skipping a reward is a real choice, and often the right one.

Pick a plan early, then protect it

Strong decks usually lean on one idea: a pile of small attacks, a strong defence that wears opponents down, or a combination of two cards that feed each other. Once you have found yours, favour cards that support it and pass on those that pull in another direction.

Balance attack and defence

A deck that only hits hard can lose to a single bad draw. Always keep a few reliable ways to absorb damage, so you can take a risk on the cards that win fights quickly.

Read the enemy before you play

Most games show what an opponent plans to do next. Spend a moment on it every turn. Often the best play is to answer the threat rather than to deal the most damage.

Spend your resources deliberately

Energy, mana and similar resources are the budget for every turn. Wasting them on weak plays makes later turns harder, and holding them back for no reason does the same.

Learn from every defeat

When a run ends, ask what actually decided it. It is usually one early choice rather than the last fight, and spotting it is how you improve.

Which One Should You Pick?

  • You want the deepest, most celebrated challenge: Slay the Spire is the benchmark, and a one-time purchase means no interruptions.
  • You like poker or chasing high scores: Balatro is a very different take on the genre and well worth its price.
  • You like clever combinations and positioning: Monster Train is the strategist’s choice.
  • You want something light and fast: Dicey Dungeons costs the least of the paid options and is easy to pick up.
  • You want to try before paying: Wildfrost lets you play until the first boss for free.
  • You want to collect creatures for free, with no ads, and duel friends: Card Bash is built around exactly that.

All of these are at their best in short sessions. Whichever you choose, the most important thing is a game that respects your time: no pressure to pay, no interruptions between fights, and a deck that feels like yours.
