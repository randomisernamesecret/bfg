Cognithix vs Atom Learning: Which 11+ Option Fits Your Family in 2026?

September 2026 · 8 min read · Education

If you're preparing a child for the 11+, Atom Learning is hard to miss. It's one of the best-known names in UK entrance exam preparation and says it is trusted by more than 350,000 families. Cognithix is a much smaller and quite different product: an iPhone and iPad app that keeps everything on the device and is paid for once. Both can help a child prepare well. They make different trade-offs, and those trade-offs matter more than any feature list.

Full disclosure: we make Cognithix. We've tried hard to be fair to Atom Learning, and everything we say about it comes from its own website and App Store listing as they stood in September 2026. Prices and plans change, so check both before you decide. For a wider look at books, apps and tutors, see our 11+ preparation guide for 2026.

The Short Version

  • Choose Atom Learning if you want maths covered in the same place, practice on a computer as well as a phone or tablet, progress synced between devices, or one service that carries on past the 11+ towards GCSEs.
  • Choose Cognithix if you want English and reasoning practice on an iPhone or iPad with no account, no monthly bill and no data leaving the device, or if your child's school offers places through a languages aptitude test.
  • Pair Cognithix with a maths resource if your child's test includes maths. It doesn't cover maths at all.

Atom Learning at a Glance

Atom Learning is an online learning platform for children aged 7 to 16. It began in the browser and, since August 2026, also has an iPhone and iPad app for families with an Atom account. Here's what it offers:

  • Subjects: English, maths and science, with verbal and non-verbal reasoning added on its exam preparation plans
  • Exams: grammar school 11+ tests, independent school entrance exams, ISEB Common Pre-Tests and CAT4, and later GCSEs
  • Adaptive practice: questions adjust to your child's level, with personalised weekly plans
  • Mock tests: unlimited online mock tests on its top plan
  • Parent view: a Track page where parents can follow progress
  • Devices: any computer or tablet with a browser, plus the iPhone and iPad app, with progress synced through your account
  • Price: £39.99 a month for English, maths and science; £59.99 a month for Exam Prep, which adds verbal and non-verbal reasoning; £69.99 a month for Exam Prep Plus, which adds unlimited mock tests. Paying yearly saves 20%, so Exam Prep works out at £575.90 a year.

It's a polished, well-established service, and its breadth is a genuine strength. One subscription can take a child from primary practice through the 11+ and on to GCSE revision.

Cognithix at a Glance

Cognithix is an iPhone and iPad app for children aged 9 to 11 who are preparing for the 11+. Here's what it offers:

  • Subjects: English (vocabulary, spelling, grammar, punctuation, comprehension and cloze), verbal reasoning (11 topics), non-verbal reasoning (8 topics) and language aptitude
  • No maths: maths is deliberately left out
  • Daily plans: a short plan suggests what to practise next and varies the topics from day to day
  • Adaptive practice: difficulty adapts during practice, and questions come with hints and an explanation of the answer
  • Timed tests: full papers, half papers, speed rounds and themed tests for each subject
  • Motivation: practice earns coins for a virtual pet, plus stars, badges and streaks
  • Parent view: a profile for each child with a weekly snapshot of time, sessions and accuracy, which topics are mastered or due for review, and insights into patterns such as rushed answers or a falling trend
  • Account and data: no account at all; profiles and progress are stored only on the device and never sent to us
  • Price: free for 7 days, then a one-time £9.99 unlock that covers every child profile. No subscription and no ads.

It's a newer and smaller product than Atom with a narrower job: short daily English and reasoning practice on a phone or tablet, with as little admin for parents as possible.

The subject row is the one to read first. Most grammar school tests include maths, and Atom covers it while Cognithix does not. If maths is on your child's paper, Cognithix can only ever be part of the answer.

Where Atom Learning Is Stronger

Maths and science. Atom covers maths alongside English and reasoning, so a single subscription can handle a typical four-subject 11+. With Cognithix you'll need workbooks or another tool for maths.

Computers and syncing. Atom works in a browser on Windows laptops, Chromebooks and Android tablets as well as on Apple devices, and progress follows your child from one to another. Cognithix runs only on iPhone and iPad, and each device keeps its own progress.

Breadth beyond the 11+. Atom also prepares children for ISEB Common Pre-Tests, CAT4 and independent school exams, and later for GCSEs. If you want one service for several years and several exams, that continuity is valuable.

Scale and track record. A large user base means plenty of parent reviews and forum discussion to learn from before you commit, which a newer app simply hasn't had time to build.

Where Cognithix Is Stronger

Cost over a long preparation. Many families prepare for 12 to 18 months. Cognithix costs the same whether your child uses it for a month or two years, and one unlock covers every child profile on the device.

No account and no data leaving the device. There's no email or password to set up. Profiles, answers and progress stay on the iPhone or iPad and are never sent to us, and a Parent PIN keeps settings and purchases in grown-up hands. The trade-off is that progress doesn't move between devices.

No connection needed. Practice works without an internet connection, which helps on journeys and in places with patchy Wi-Fi.

Language aptitude practice. A small number of schools with a languages specialism offer some Year 7 places through a languages aptitude test. Cognithix has a whole subject for this kind of task, from sound clues and word matching to number learning and sentence building. Atom doesn't list it as a subject. Our guide to language aptitude tests explains who sets them.

Built around a short daily habit. The daily plan, the pet that needs looking after and the quick sessions are designed for ten or fifteen minutes a day on a phone or tablet. Atom has its own rewards and milestones too, so this comes down to which style your child responds to.

Worth doing the sums: at Atom's Exam Prep price, a year costs £719.88 paid monthly or £575.90 paid yearly. Cognithix costs £9.99 once, but if your child's test includes maths you'll also need a maths resource, such as a set of workbooks. Compare the whole package, not just the headline price.

What Neither App Does

No app replaces wide reading, which builds the vocabulary that sits under English and verbal reasoning scores. Neither app can tell you the exact format of your child's test either. That comes from the school or consortium, many of which publish free familiarisation papers, so get those first. And if your child's test includes a written task, that practice is best done with pen and paper, whichever app you choose.

It's also worth checking which test your child will actually sit, because the landscape has shifted. Our guide to GL vs CEM in 2026 explains what changed and how to check.

Which Should You Choose?

Atom Learning is likely the better fit if...

  • Your child's test includes maths and you want everything in one place
  • Your child practises on a computer, Chromebook or Android tablet
  • You want progress to follow your child between devices
  • You'd like one service that continues beyond the 11+

Cognithix is likely the better fit if...

  • Your family uses iPhones or iPads and wants short daily practice
  • You'd rather pay once than take on a monthly subscription
  • You don't want to create an account or store your child's data online
  • Your child's target school offers places through a languages aptitude test
  • You're already covering maths with workbooks or a tutor

A budget route that covers everything

Cognithix for English, reasoning and language aptitude, plus a set of maths workbooks from Bond or CGP, covers a typical four-subject 11+ for less than a single month of the larger subscriptions.

Final Thoughts

Atom Learning and Cognithix are both reasonable ways to prepare. Atom is broader, covers maths and syncs across devices, and you pay for that every month. Cognithix is narrower, private by design and paid for once, and it expects you to handle maths another way. Neither is a shortcut. Regular short practice, plenty of reading, enough sleep and a calm home matter more than any app.

Both offer a free trial, so the simplest test is to let your child try each for a few days and watch which one they return to without being asked. The best tool is the one your child will actually use, steadily, over the months ahead.
