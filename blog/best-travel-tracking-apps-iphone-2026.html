Best Travel Tracking Apps for iPhone in 2026: Manual, Automatic and Photo-Based

September 2026 · 9 min read · Travel

There is something deeply satisfying about a map of everywhere you have been. Whether it is a scratch-off poster on the wall or an interactive globe on your phone, tracking your travels turns fleeting memories into a record you can return to. But the way an app tracks matters, both for what you get out of it and for how much of your life it records along the way.

This guide covers the travel tracking apps worth knowing on iPhone in 2026, grouped by how they work: apps you fill in yourself, apps that follow your location, and apps that build your history from photos you have already taken. Plans and prices change often, so we describe how each one charges rather than quoting exact amounts.

Three Ways to Track Your Travels

A good travel tracker gives you three things: an overview of where you have been, a timeline you can browse by date, and a way to relive individual trips. Apps get there in one of three ways:

  • Manual: you tick off countries or log trips yourself. Full control, no location tracking, but only as complete as your memory and discipline.
  • Automatic: the app records your location in the background. Effortless and detailed, but it creates a continuous record of your movements.
  • Photo-based: the app reads the location and date already stored in your photos. Nothing new is recorded, and past trips appear straight away.

Manual Trackers and Travel Journals

Visited

Visited is one of the best-known country trackers. You mark the countries you have been to, lived in or want to visit, and it colours in a map and gives you travel statistics. It includes over 150 themed travel lists, from World Heritage sites to famous film locations, and you can compare maps with friends. The core app is free; Premium, sold as a subscription or a lifetime purchase, adds city tracking and regions for more than 30 countries. Visited does not track your location; it knows only what you tell it.

Polarsteps

Polarsteps is the most polished travel journal around. You plan a trip, and while it is active the app records your route in the background and lets you add photos and notes to each stop. Friends and family can follow along, and afterwards you can order a printed travel book laid out from your trip. Planning, tracking and sharing are free; books cost extra, and since July 2026 an optional Polarsteps Plus subscription adds 3D maps, extra map styles, journey replays and more detailed statistics. Trips are stored in your Polarsteps account. For a closer look, see our PhotoFlight vs Polarsteps comparison.

TripIt

TripIt builds itineraries from the confirmation emails you forward to it: flights, hotels, car hire. It is excellent for keeping a complicated trip in one place, and the paid Pro tier adds alerts and extras for frequent travellers. It records where you planned to go rather than where you actually went, so it is an organiser first and a travel map second.

Flighty

If flights are what you want to count, Flighty is worth a look. It is best known as a live flight tracker with fast delay alerts, but its Passport screen keeps a lifetime record of the flights you have logged: distance flown, hours in the air, airports, routes and aircraft types. The free version includes tracking and the Passport; Flighty Pro, sold by subscription or as a lifetime purchase, adds your full flight history, calendar sync and imports from email and TripIt. It covers flights only, not the places you spent time on the ground.

Automatic Location Trackers

Google Maps Timeline

For years, Google Maps Timeline quietly recorded the places you visited and the routes between them. In 2025 Google moved Timeline from its servers onto your phone: the web version was retired, your history now lives on the device, and an optional encrypted backup lets you move it to a new phone. That is a real privacy improvement. The trade-offs are that Timeline is tied to the Google Maps app on one device, and recent defaults delete older history automatically unless you change the setting. As a detailed daily record it is still unmatched; as a lifelong travel archive it needs some care.

Arc Timeline

Arc Timeline is the iPhone favourite for people who want a detailed diary of every place and every journey. It records continuously in the background, works out whether you were walking, cycling or driving, and learns your regular places over time. Data stays on your device by default. In 2026 its developer released Arc Timeline 4, a ground-up rebuild, and it is sold by subscription or as a lifetime purchase. The cost is constant background location access, with some battery use, and an app that knows your complete movement history.

Worth asking before you install: where is my location history stored, on my device or on the company's servers? Can I export it if the app disappears? And if the company were breached, what would someone learn about where I go?

The Photo-Based Approach

There is a middle way between logging everything by hand and being tracked all day. Every photo you take on a smartphone normally records where and when it was taken. Your camera roll is already a detailed, timestamped record of your travels, so no new tracking is needed.

PhotoFlight, our own app, takes this approach. It reads the dates and locations in your photo library and builds a 3D globe, a world map, a travel passport and multi-stop trips, with albums for every trip, country and city. Transport between places is estimated from what your photos show, and you can correct anything it gets wrong. There is no account and no background location; your travel history is stored on your device, while Apple's services look up place names and draw the maps. It is free to download, with a one-time Pro purchase for your full photo history, trip videos and other extras. The limitation is the obvious one: it only knows about places where you took photos.

What to Look for in a Travel Tracking App

  • Past trips: does the app only start from today, or can it show the trips you took before installing it?
  • Offline access: you will want your maps while travelling, which is often when your connection is worst.
  • Export: if the app shuts down or you switch phones, can you take your history with you?
  • Privacy model: on-device only, on the company's servers, or synced through your own cloud account? For location data, this matters more than almost anything else.
  • Pricing: free, subscription or one-time purchase? A tracker is a long-term habit, so think about what it will cost over several years.
  • Effort: be honest about whether you will really log trips or remember to start tracking before you leave.

Which Approach Is Right for You?

If you simply want to colour in a world map and count countries, Visited is the easiest place to start. If you want a rich, shareable journal of an upcoming trip, with a printed book at the end, Polarsteps is the most polished choice. If your trips are mostly business and bookings, TripIt keeps them organised, and Flighty is the specialist for counting flights. If you want a detailed record of every day and are comfortable with background location, Google Maps Timeline or Arc Timeline will capture far more than any other approach.

If you would rather not be tracked at all, but still want a full picture of where you have been, a photo-based app gives you years of history from data you already have. Our guide to building a travel map from your photos explains how that data works and how to make it more complete on your next trip.

The best travel tracker is the one that fits your habits. If you will not remember to log trips, a manual app will sit unused. If background tracking makes you uneasy, an always-on app will be deleted within a week. Choose for how you actually travel, not how you wish you did.
