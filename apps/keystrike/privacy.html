Keystrike

Privacy Policy

Privacy Policy Support Terms of Use

Last updated: 19 November 2026

Keystrike is designed to teach piano practice without collecting personal data. This policy explains what stays on your device and how the App Store unlock is handled. Parts marked “from version 1.6” apply to that version and later.

Summary

Keystrike does not require an account, does not include advertising SDKs, and does not transmit practice data to Blue Flame Games. Progress, recordings, imports, settings, and lesson data stay on your device. Nothing leaves it unless you choose to send it yourself, for example with the system share sheet (see Sharing and Exporting).

Data Stored on Your Device

  • Lesson progress, completed grades, and unlocked achievements
  • Streak data, practice history, and weak-spot statistics
  • Appearance, tempo, hand-filter, metronome, and reminder preferences, and, per learner profile, whether the Quick body check is on and the day it was last answered
  • Calibrated audio offset
  • Learner profiles you add (a name and an avatar) and the keyboard setups you save
  • Free-piano recordings you have saved, stored as note data (which keys, when, and how firmly), never as sound, with the title you give them and, if you answer the short self-check, your answers
  • Imported MIDI files (.mid / .midi): the practice chart Keystrike makes from each file, and a reference to where the file is. The file itself is not copied.
  • From version 1.6, imported piano recordings: a copy of the recording kept inside the app, and the practice chart of notes Keystrike hears in it
  • From version 1.6, recordings you bring to Jam: a copy of each recording kept inside the app, a small notes file of plain numbers about it (no sound), and an entry with the name of your file, when you added it, its length, and the key you chose. Your best scores and stars on each recording are kept with your progress, and Keystrike remembers, for each learner profile, the last Jam intensity (Chill, Groove or Wild) you chose
  • Safety copies of the data above, which Keystrike makes on the device before an app update and before a restore from a Local Backup

All of the above is stored locally in the standard iOS or macOS app container and is encrypted by the operating system when your device locks. See Deletion for how it goes away.

Imported Piano Recordings (from version 1.6)

You can import a recording of piano playing from the Files app. Keystrike makes a copy of the file inside the app, on your device, and listens to that copy on your device to find the notes you will play along with. The notes become a practice chart, kept like your other imports.

Keystrike keeps the copy so that it can play your recording back to you while you practise with it, and keeps it until you delete the import. The copy is never uploaded, and it is never part of a share card, a Local Backup file, a practice report, or any other file Keystrike makes. Keystrike never changes or deletes the original file you picked. While an import is running, the copy is held in a temporary place inside the app; if you cancel, or the import fails or is refused, that copy is removed and nothing is kept.

Keystrike marks the copy so that your device and iCloud backups leave it out. The notes of your import are part of Keystrike’s other data, so after you restore a device from a backup the import is still there, and its notes play through Keystrike’s own piano instead of your recording.

Deleting the import in Keystrike removes the copy straight away. If a copy is ever left without its import (for example after a learner profile is deleted), Keystrike removes it the next time it opens. See Deletion for removing the app.

Jam Recordings (from version 1.6)

Jam makes a part for you to play along with a recording you bring to it from the Files app. Keystrike makes a copy of the file inside the app, on your device, and listens to that copy on your device to find where its strong hits and beats fall, how it divides into sections, and the key it seems to be in. None of this is sent anywhere.

Jam keeps two files inside the app, on your device: the copy of your recording, and a small notes file about it. The notes file holds plain numbers only (how loud the recording is from moment to moment, where its strong hits and beats fall, its tempo, its sections, the key it seems to be in and, where Keystrike can tell, which note the bass rests on at regular points) and none of its sound. Keystrike keeps the copy so that it can play your recording back to you during your Jam runs and in the key preview, and keeps the notes file so that starting a run, or changing the key, does not mean listening again. Both stay until you delete the recording from Jam. If the notes file is ever missing or damaged, Keystrike listens to the copy again and makes it afresh.

Keystrike also keeps a short entry for each recording in its saved data: the name of your file, when you added it, its length, and the key you chose for it. Your best scores and stars on it are kept with your progress, and Keystrike remembers, for each learner profile, the last Jam intensity (Chill, Groove or Wild) you chose.

Neither file is ever uploaded. Neither is part of a Local Backup file, a practice report, or any other file Keystrike makes. Jam runs offer no share card and no video clip, and the share cards and clips of Keystrike’s other features never contain your recording (see Sharing and Exporting). Jam runs never use the microphone. Keystrike never changes or deletes the original file you picked. While Keystrike is listening, the copy is held in a temporary place inside the app; if you cancel, or the file is refused or fails, that copy is removed and nothing is kept.

Keystrike marks both files so that your device and iCloud backups leave them out, and if the system refused the marking, Keystrike marks them again every time it opens. The entry is part of Keystrike’s other saved data, so after you restore a device from a backup (or restore a Local Backup on another device) the recording is still listed, with its key and scores, marked “Needs your recording”. Add the recording again and it plays as before.

To remove a Jam recording, delete it from its row on the Jam shelf and confirm. Keystrike removes the copy, the notes file, your scores for it, and its entry, straight away. If a copy or notes file is ever left without its entry (for example after an interrupted delete or after a learner profile is deleted), Keystrike removes it the next time it opens. See Deletion for removing the app.

Quick Body Check (from version 1.6)

The Quick body check is an optional reminder about posture: feet, back, wrists and fingers. It is off until you switch on “Quick body check” in Settings, in the Practice section, and you can switch it off again at any time. When it is on, it appears at most once a day, before your first lesson or Practice run; it never appears before an Arcade, Band, or Jam run.

It is a list of four tick boxes that you tick yourself, by looking at your own hands and posture. It uses no camera, no microphone, no motion or other sensor, and it takes no picture or measurement of you. Keystrike keeps only two things about it, for each learner profile, on your device: whether it is on, and the day you last answered it (by ticking all four boxes or choosing “Skip for now”). Nothing about it is uploaded or sent to Blue Flame Games.

Legal Section in Settings (from version 1.6)

Settings ends with a Legal section. It holds three links, Privacy, Terms, and Support, which open the pages of this site (this policy, the Terms of Use, and the Support page) in your web browser, and an Acknowledgements screen. Acknowledgements lists the credits and licence notices for the music, the piano sound, the notation fonts, and the open-source software that Keystrike includes. The screen is built into the app, shows only text, and works without a network connection. Opening one of the three links is the same as visiting the page in your browser: your browser connects to this website, as it would for any web page. Keystrike itself sends nothing when you tap it.

Imported MIDI Files

If you import your own MIDI file from the Files app, Keystrike reads the file from the location you chose and turns it into a practice chart, which it keeps inside the app. It also keeps a reference to where the file is. The file itself is not copied, and the original remains where you put it. Keystrike plays the chart it made, so it does not need the original again. Nothing about your imports is uploaded.

Recordings You Make

When you record yourself playing and choose to save the take, Keystrike stores it as a list of notes: which key, when, and how firmly. It stores no sound. This holds whether you played on a MIDI keyboard, on the on-screen keys, or on an acoustic piano in acoustic mode (then it holds the notes Keystrike heard, never the sound). A saved recording stays on your device and plays back through Keystrike’s own piano. You can delete it from My recordings at any time, or export it as a MIDI file yourself (see Sharing and Exporting).

Microphone (Acoustic-Piano Mode)

Keystrike includes an optional acoustic-piano mode that listens through the device microphone to detect notes you play on a real piano. It is off until you turn on “Use the microphone” in Settings and allow microphone access. It listens only while Keystrike is open, only on screens that take notes (such as lessons, practice, and free piano), and not while a MIDI keyboard is connected. The microphone is also used when you choose to set up an acoustic piano by ear. Arcade and Jam runs never use it.

All listening happens on your device. In lessons and free-piano play, the sound is processed in real time to estimate the pitch of each note and is not recorded; only the detected notes are used, and they are kept only if you save a recording of that play (as notes, never as sound). When you play a whole piece in acoustic mode, Keystrike records the take to a temporary file in the app’s cache on your device and grades it there after you finish. That file is deleted as soon as grading ends or you cancel, and any file left behind by an interrupted take is deleted the next time Keystrike opens. The optional room check measures loudness for a few seconds, shows you the result, and keeps nothing. Sound from the microphone is never kept for playback and never transmitted.

MIDI Input

Keystrike receives note events from any USB or Bluetooth MIDI keyboard you have connected. These events are processed locally and used to drive lesson scoring. Nothing about your playing is sent off the device by Keystrike.

Sharing and Exporting

Keystrike never sends anything by itself. The features below make a file on your device, and it leaves the device only when you pick a destination in the system share sheet or save it yourself. Blue Flame Games never receives it.

  • Share card (from version 1.6): a picture of a run’s result, offered on the results page of a whole run in which at least one note landed. It shows the piece’s title (for an import, the title you gave it or the name of your file), the level, your stars, score, and combo, and, for a piece from Keystrike’s library, its composer. It shows your first name and avatar only if you switch on “Show my name” for that card, which is off every time. The caption is prefilled, you can edit it, and it includes a link to Keystrike’s App Store page. A card is a picture only: it never contains imported audio or any sound of your own. Nothing is posted automatically. Keystrike deletes the card’s temporary file when you close the share sheet.
  • Share clip (from version 1.6): a 15-second video of your own Arcade run, made on your device, and only when you tap “Make a video of your best moment”, which is offered after a run with at least five scored hits (and not for the few library pieces that need a credit). While you play, Keystrike keeps a short list in the device’s memory of what happened in the run (which key, when, how firmly, and for how long, plus pauses and tempo changes). It holds no sound and no picture, it is never written to storage, it takes nothing from the screen, the microphone, or the camera, and it is dropped when you close the results or start the next run (or, if you tapped Share, when you close the share sheet). The clip is drawn from that list, only after you ask for it. It shows the stage as you played it, the piece’s title (for an import, the title you gave it or the name of your file), and an end card like the share card above. That card shows your first name and avatar only if “Show my name” is on; it is off every time you open the sheet. A clip’s sound is made only from Keystrike’s own sounds: Keystrike’s piano playing the keys you pressed and, for a piece from Keystrike’s library, that piece’s own track and, if your run had one, its band. It never contains the sound of a recording or MIDI file you imported, and never anything from the microphone; for a piece you imported or a recording you saved, Keystrike’s own piano plays the notes. The video is a temporary file inside the app, and it leaves the device only when you pick a destination in the system share sheet, together with a prefilled caption that you can edit and that includes a link to Keystrike’s App Store page. Nothing is posted automatically. Keystrike deletes the file when the system share sheet closes, when you close Keystrike’s share sheet or the results page, and at once if you cancel the making of the clip or it fails, and it removes any file left there for more than 15 minutes when the app opens or goes to the background.
  • MIDI file of a recording: one of your saved recordings, as notes only.
  • Practice report, weekly summary, and recital card: a summary of your practice as a PDF, text, or picture file.
  • Local Backup: a file of Keystrike’s saved data (your progress, recordings, the practice charts of your imports, and the list of your Jam recordings with your scores). It never contains imported audio files, the copy of a recording you brought to Jam, or the notes file made from it, and it leaves out your purchase and trial status.

Notifications

Keystrike can send a single daily-practice reminder at a time you choose. Notifications are scheduled by iOS or macOS and never reach a Blue Flame Games server. You can disable them in iOS or macOS Settings at any time.

Trial and In-App Purchase

Keystrike uses Apple's App Store purchase system for the optional one-time unlock after the trial. Purchases, refunds, Family Sharing eligibility, and payment records are handled by Apple. Blue Flame Games does not receive your card number or App Store password.

Network Use

Keystrike is local-first. The app does not upload practice data, recordings, imports, recordings you bring to Jam, or microphone audio. The App Store purchase system may contact Apple to show products, purchase the unlock, restore purchases, and validate entitlement state.

When you open the “More from Blue Flame Games” screen and it has no saved list yet or its saved list is more than a week old (or your device’s country has changed), Keystrike asks Apple’s public App Store lookup for the current names and icons of our other apps; the request contains only those apps’ App Store IDs and your device’s country code. Keystrike then downloads any new icons from Apple.

What We Do NOT Do

  • No accounts, no sign-up, no email collection
  • No advertising, no advertising SDKs, no advertising identifiers
  • No analytics, no telemetry, no crash-reporting service
  • No third-party SDKs of any kind
  • No location collection
  • No upload of microphone audio, MIDI input, recordings, imported piano recordings, recordings you bring to Jam, or imported MIDI files
  • No data sold or shared with anyone, ever; what you choose to send, you send yourself

Children

Keystrike is suitable for children. Because it collects no personal information and sends nothing about your playing off the device by itself, the question of children's data simply does not arise. Sharing a result card or a clip always starts with a deliberate tap on Share and ends in the system share sheet, and on a device with several profiles, a profile marked as a child’s has sharing off until a grown-up turns it on. Parents can hand a child the app with the same confidence as a printed book.

Deletion

You can delete a saved recording, an import, or a Jam recording inside Keystrike at any time. Deleting an imported piano recording removes its practice chart and the copy of the recording kept in the app. Deleting a Jam recording removes the copy of the recording, its notes file, your scores for it, and its entry. Deleting a learner profile removes that profile’s progress, recordings, imports, and Jam recordings, including from the safety copies. The copies of that profile’s imported and Jam recordings kept in the app are removed the next time Keystrike opens.

Uninstalling Keystrike from an iPhone or iPad removes the app and everything it stored on the device: progress, recordings, settings, imports (with every copy of an imported piano recording), Jam recordings (with their copies and notes files), and safety copies. On a Mac, delete your imports and Jam recordings inside Keystrike before you remove the app, because macOS may leave an app’s data folder in your Library after the app is moved to the Trash. The only thing kept is the free trial's dates, which are stored in the device's keychain so the trial survives a reinstall. Files you shared or exported yourself (a card, a clip, a report, a backup, or a MIDI file) are copies you sent or saved, so remove them where you put them. Because nothing was ever uploaded, no further deletion request is necessary.
