Ask, and the source comes with the answer

Type a question in plain English, such as “Apple income statement” or “Compare AAPL vs MSFT”. Kanesh finds the company, fetches the data and shows it as a card that links to its source. If it isn't sure which company you mean, it asks.

What it does

A research companion for the stocks you follow: ask a question, get a card, open the source. All AI runs on your device.

Plain-English questions

Ask for a price, a statement, a comparison or a rate in your own words. Kanesh finds the company, fetches the data and shows a card that links to its source. When it isn't sure which company you mean, it asks.

Sources you can open

No account or key needed. Prices, history, company data and news come from Yahoo Finance and SEC EDGAR. FRED (economic data) and Finnhub (extra company data, earnings dates, insider trades) are optional free keys.

Prices in your currency

Each price is converted correctly from its listing's currency, so London pence, Tokyo yen and US dollars sit side by side.

Financials & filings

Income statement, balance sheet and cash flow come straight from SEC EDGAR filings, with links to 10-K, 10-Q and 8-K filings.

Markets & watchlist

Commodities in real units (per barrel, ounce, bushel), currencies, a sector heat map and a fund explorer. Rates, inflation, jobs data and Treasury yields need a free FRED key. The free watchlist holds five tickers, with price alerts.

Kanesh Pro

Unlimited watchlist with position sizes, a portfolio exposure map by sector and country, side-by-side comparisons, a daily Morning Brief, event prep for central-bank meetings (and earnings dates with a Finnhub key), investment theses, a decision journal and smart alerts for new SEC filings. A one-time lifetime purchase or a monthly subscription.

Private by design

Kanesh has no account and no analytics. All AI runs on your device, so your questions never leave it; data sources receive only what they need, such as a ticker symbol.

  • No account, no sign-up, no ads.
  • All AI runs on the device.
  • Data requests carry only what the data needs, such as a ticker symbol.
  • Free data-provider keys you add (FRED, Finnhub) are stored on your device and used only with that provider.

Who it's for

Kanesh is for individual investors, students and curious people who follow a handful of companies and want to check what they read against the source, on a phone. It fits the intent behind searches like stock research app for iPhone, SEC filings app and Bloomberg alternative for individual investors, as long as you want a research companion rather than a trading terminal.

Primary sources first

The point is not only to show a number. It is to show where the number came from, so you can open the filing or dataset and read it yourself.

Made for the phone

Watchlist, headlines, markets and filings laid out for a small screen, with questions you can type instead of menus to learn.

Clear about limits

Coverage, freshness and delays vary by provider, and some sources need your own free key. Check the date on a figure before you act on it.

Named for old tablets

Kaneš, in Kültepe, central Anatolia, was an Old Assyrian trading colony (c. 1950–1750 BC). Its merchants left about 23,000 clay tablets, among the oldest surviving business records. Kanesh keeps the habit: every number links to its source.

Frequently asked questions

Can I verify the data myself?

Yes, and you should. Result cards name their source and link to it, and filings open on SEC EDGAR. Kanesh presents financial information, not investment advice.

Is Kanesh only for US markets?

No. Prices cover stocks, ETFs, funds, crypto and commodities on many exchanges, shown in your own currency. Company financials and filings come from SEC EDGAR, so they cover companies that file with the SEC.

A Bloomberg alternative on my phone?

For individual research it covers useful ground: charts, filings, news and economic data with the sources in view. It is not a professional terminal and it does not place trades. The guides below compare the options.
