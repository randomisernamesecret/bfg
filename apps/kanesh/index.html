Tickview: SEC Filings & Alerts

Sourced alerts for your watchlist and the economy. Tickview tells you about new SEC filings, insider purchases and price crossings, and shows Recession Watch and the bond market with their sources and data dates. It opens on a Today tab, and widgets, Siri and Shortcuts show the same sourced figures outside the app. Information only, and all the AI runs on your device.

Alerts from the source

Tickview watches your watchlist and the economy in the background and tells you when something new is published. Every alert and card names its source and shows when its data is from. Alerts are information, and they can arrive late.

What it does

A companion for the companies you follow and the state of the economy: a Today tab, alerts, model estimates, figures for your own holdings and sourced cards, each with the date of its data. All AI runs on your device. Tickview gives information, never advice.

Today

The tab Tickview opens on. It gathers the morning brief, what your alerts caught, the dated events coming up, the Recession Watch status and your watchlist's moves, each with its source and date. The tabs are Today, Ask, Portfolio and Markets, with Settings behind the gear.

Alerts in the background

Price alerts trigger once when a price crosses your level, in your own currency. Tickview Pro adds alerts for new SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, offering documents), insider purchases reported on SEC Form 4, earnings dates (with a free Finnhub key) and changes in recession indicators. Alerts are checked when iOS allows, are information, and can arrive late. One Alerts screen lists everything being watched.

Recession Watch

The model probability of a US recession within 12 months, from the yield-curve model published by the New York Fed and calculated by Tickview from Treasury data, shown with how often that figure has been right, false alarms included. It also tracks the 10-year minus 3-month and 10-year minus 2-year curves, re-steepening after an inversion and the Sahm rule, each with its formula, data date and track record. It shows a count of active warning indicators, never a blended score, and works with no API key. Pro adds history since 1960 with recessions shaded.

Bond market

Today's Treasury curve against a month and a year ago, how unusual the latest 30-day move is compared with history since 1962, and real yields and breakeven inflation.

Numbers checked

AI summaries are compared with the source figures before they are shown. If one fails the check, a plain factual sentence is shown instead. Every card also shows when its data is from, such as “Market close · Fri 16:00 ET”.

Sourced answers

Ask in plain English for a price, a statement, a comparison or a rate and get a card that links to its source. Tickview asks when it is unsure which company you mean, and a follow-up such as “and its debt?” continues the same thread. Company financials come straight from SEC EDGAR and go back up to 15 fiscal years, with every period linked to the SEC filing it came from. Answers are kept on your device, so they still show offline with the time they were fetched, and when a source is down Tickview says which one. Prices are converted correctly from each listing's currency.

Holdings, gain and loss

Enter your shares and cost and see each position's value. Tickview Pro adds totals, day change and gain/loss in your own currency, and a price history can be shown in your currency at each date's exchange rate. Every total says which positions it covers, and a missing cost, price or rate shows as “—”, never as zero. These are estimates from the prices shown and the numbers you typed, not an account statement and not tax figures.

Widgets

A Watchlist widget for the Home Screen and the Lock Screen, a Recession Watch widget and, with Tickview Pro, a widget with your choice of tickers. Every figure carries its time and its source, and a price Tickview has not re-checked is dimmed and labelled “stale”.

Siri, Shortcuts and Spotlight

Ask Tickview a question, open a ticker or get the Recession Watch status through Siri and Shortcuts. Answers are worked out on your device and come with their source and the time of their data. Your tickers, theses and journal notes can be found in Spotlight search, from an index that stays on the device.

Event prep and the morning brief

Event prep lists what is scheduled for your watchlist in the next 14 days: earnings dates, CPI, the jobs report, GDP and central-bank decisions, each with where its date came from. The morning brief is built only from dated, sourced inputs: moves, new filings, what is scheduled, the bond and recession figures and attributed headlines. Both are part of Tickview Pro.

Share cards

Turn a result into an image to share: the figures, their source, the date of the data and the source's link. A card never carries your share counts, position values or notes, and like everything in Tickview it is information only.

Markets & watchlist

Commodities, currencies, a sector map and a fund explorer. The free watchlist holds five tickers. Tickview Pro adds an unlimited watchlist, holdings totals with gain/loss, a widget with your choice of tickers, an exposure map, comparisons, a daily brief, event prep, theses and a journal. New users get Pro features for 7 days in the app with no payment details; after that Pro is a one-time lifetime purchase or a monthly subscription.

Private by design

Tickview has no account, no ads and no tracking. All AI runs on your device, so your questions never leave it. Background checks fetch public data only, and nothing about you is uploaded.

  • No account, no sign-up, no ads.
  • All AI runs on the device.
  • Data requests carry only what the data needs, such as a ticker symbol, and there is no Blue Flame Games server.
  • Data comes from Yahoo Finance, SEC EDGAR, the U.S. Treasury, the Bureau of Labor Statistics and the Bureau of Economic Analysis with no key needed. Free optional keys you add (FRED, Finnhub) are stored on your device and used only with that provider. The privacy policy lists every service Tickview contacts.
  • Your holdings, the figures your widgets show and the Spotlight entries for your tickers and notes stay on the device. A share card leaves it only when you share one.

Who it's for

Tickview is for individual investors, students and curious people who follow a handful of companies and the economy, and want to check what they read against the source, on a phone. It fits the intent behind searches like SEC filings alerts, recession indicators, yield curve tracker and stock research app for iPhone, as long as you want an information app rather than a trading terminal.

Primary sources first

The point is not only to show a number. It is to show where the number came from, so you can open the filing or dataset and read it yourself.

Made for the phone

Alerts, watchlist, markets and filings laid out for a small screen, with questions you can type instead of menus to learn. On iPad, Tickview uses a sidebar layout, and it works with VoiceOver and larger text sizes.

Clear about limits

Coverage, freshness and delays vary by provider, alerts can arrive late, and some sources need your own free key. Every card shows the date of its data. Tickview gives information, not advice.

Why Tickview

A tick is the smallest move a price can make. Tickview shows each number as it is: where it came from, when it was read, and a link to the original page. The decision stays yours.

Frequently asked questions

Can I verify the data myself?

Yes. Result cards name their source, link to it and show the date of their data, and filings open on SEC EDGAR. Tickview presents financial information, not investment advice.

Is Tickview only for US markets?

No. Prices cover stocks, ETFs, funds, crypto and commodities on many exchanges, shown in your own currency. Company financials and filings come from SEC EDGAR, so they cover companies that file with the SEC.

Are the alerts and Recession Watch predictions?

No. Recession Watch shows a model probability, the model estimate published by the New York Fed and calculated by Tickview from Treasury data, together with how often it has been right and its false alarms. Alerts are information, are checked when iOS allows and can arrive late. Neither is a prediction or a prompt to trade. The guides below cover the sources.

Are the gain and loss figures tax figures?

No. Values, gains and losses are estimates, calculated on your device from third-party prices and exchange rates and from the share counts and costs you typed. They are not a statement of any account and not figures for a tax return. Your broker's or provider's records are the record.

Do widgets, Siri and Spotlight send my data anywhere?

No. Widgets read a small copy of your watchlist's figures that Tickview keeps on the device. Answers through Siri and Shortcuts are worked out on the device; when you speak to Siri, Apple handles the voice request under its own privacy policy. The Spotlight index for your tickers and notes stays on the device.

What does a share card show?

Public figures with their source, the date of the data and the source's link. It never shows your share counts, position values or notes. A card is made on your device and leaves it only when you choose to share it.
